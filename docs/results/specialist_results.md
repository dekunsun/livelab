## Reading, against the rules fixed before the run

*The tables above are generated and unedited. This section was written after the results were
known.*

**Opus 5's S+L is not read.** Anthropic credit ran out after 79 of its 100 items, and 79% is
below the 90% coverage this project reads. Its row counts the 21 missing items as wrong, and it
is shown only for completeness. Ps3 is judged on Gemini alone until the remaining items run.

### Ps1 not met: the general model beat the specialist in the specialist's best case

A 5-nearest-neighbour specialist on frozen DINOv2 features, trained on 2,474 of this lab's own
annotations, got **65 of 100**. That rises to 67 with near-duplicates excluded more strictly, so
the score is not memory of the bench. Gemini, looking at each photograph with the check in hand
and never having seen this lab, got **74**. The registration fixed the reading of this outcome
in advance: *the general model beats the specialist even where the specialist should be
strongest.* Two limits govern it:

- **It is one model's result.** Opus 5, with the same photographs, got 57, below the specialist,
  mostly because it abstained on 23 items. "Native beats specialist" holds for Gemini and not
  for Opus.
- **It is one specialist design.** A fine-tuned network would likely do better than k-NN on frozen
  features. The registration says what this means: where a general model wins, it does not show
  that every specialist would lose.

The specialist is also unsure of most of its answers. On 64 of the 100 items its vote was 3 to 2,
and it was right on 40 of those. At 5 of 5 it was right 9 times in 9.

### Ps2 met: the specialist's accuracy is bought with labels

Trained on 10% of the annotations (247), S got 31, with 29 UNKNOWN for checks too thin to vote on.
It got 42 at 25% and 59 at 50%. It never reached the native model's 74, even with all 2,474. The
native model needed none. For a lab, that is the trade in one line: a specialist costs almost
nothing to run (22 ms to embed an image on a laptop CPU, against about US$0.01 an inspection for
Opus 5), and costs thousands of labelled photographs to build.

### Ps3 not met, for Gemini: handing the verdict to a language model lost accuracy

Given only the classifier's verdict and vote count, Gemini got **51**, against S's 65. The
registered reading for this direction is that *the pipeline's weak joint is the hand-off*, and
the records show how:

- **Gemini never overrode the classifier.** It followed S on 83 items, including 32 on which S was
  wrong. It had no picture, so it had nothing to correct S with.
- **It abstained where S's vote was weak.** Seventeen UNKNOWNs, almost all on 3-to-2 votes. S was
  right on 14 of those 17.

So the language model added no information, and it turned a weak but better-than-chance signal
into silence. That abstention is not unreasonable: a 3-to-2 vote is weak evidence. But it cost
accuracy, and it is the opposite of this project's phase-one finding, where models committed
without evidence. Here the evidence was *stated* to be weak, and the model respected it. That
fits pilot 1's C0: a gap that is stated gets respected, and a gap that is implied does not.

### Ps4 not met: the specialist handles the step worse

On the 20 images labelled normal under one step and abnormal under another, S got both steps
right on **6**, against Gemini's 14. S decides each check separately, but its neighbours are
chosen by how the image looks, and the same image looks the same under both checks. So it tends
to give one image one answer. Gemini reads the check and the picture together.

### What this adds, and what it does not

For this lab's inspection photographs, **a general multimodal model with the task in hand beat a
standard specialist trained on the lab's own data**. It did so without labels, and it was better
exactly where the answer depends on the protocol step. A specialist whose verdict is passed to a
language model did worse than the specialist alone.

It does not show that native multimodal beats every vision pipeline. It is one lab, stills only,
one specialist design, and one of two native models. It says nothing about time, streaming or
live monitoring.
