## Reading, against the rules fixed before the run

*The tables above are generated by the scorer and were not edited. This section was written after
the results were known, so it applies the registration's reading table and adds nothing it did not
already allow.*

**Both models saw the frames.** Before any item ran, each one passed the frames-arrive check: it
reported 6 images when sent 6 and 0 when sent none
([evidence](../../results/vision/frames_arrived/)). The committed requests show the two arms
differ in exactly two things: the six images, and one sentence saying they are attached. So neither
result below comes from dropped attachments.

### Opus 5: the camera raises suspicion, not accuracy

The registration fixed this reading in advance: *"Detection rises and control false alerts rise as
much — the camera raises suspicion, not accuracy. Reported as such, never as detection."*

- Detection on blind rose **21 points** (12 → 16 of 19). False alerts on fault-free runs rose
  **38 points** (9 → 20 of 29), more than detection did.
- **Every flip went the same way.** The frames turned the verdict to `ANOMALOUS` on 4 blind items
  and on **11 fault-free runs**, and turned it away from `ANOMALOUS` on none.
- Over all 48 items, Opus called the run anomalous **21 times without photographs and 36 times
  with them**.

Shown six pictures of a normal column, Opus called it faulty in more than two runs out of three.
The frames did not tell it which runs were faulty. They made every run look more faulty.

### Gemini 3.8 Live: the camera changes nothing

Detection on blind moved by **−2 items**, and false alerts on control by 2 up and 2 down. Both are
under the three-item difference this project does not interpret. This matches the registered
reading for a flat result: *"Adding vision does not substitute for an instrument."*

### The predictions

- **Pr1 (a null).** Met for Gemini. For Opus it failed, but not by compensation: Pr2 failed with it,
  and by more.
- **Pr3.** Opus abstained on 2 of 19 blind items in the telemetry arm, 11% against a 10% bar. That
  is one item over, below this project's three-item rule. It is reported, not interpreted. The
  frames arm brought it down to 1 of 19.
- **Neither model** said `UNKNOWN` more often when shown photographs. As everywhere in this
  project, another modality did not make a model say it cannot tell.

### What this adds to the multimodal question

This is the second measured case where vision did not help the judgment. In the simulated arm,
micrographs added nothing (0.90 with and without). On the real plant, one model ignored the camera
and the other was made more alarmed by it on every item where it changed its answer.

For a lab deploying a camera beside its instruments, the Opus result is a warning. **A model that
becomes more willing to alarm when shown pictures will look like it gained detection**, which is
the 21 points, unless its false alerts are measured on the same footage. Without the fault-free
runs in this study, that number would have been reported as the camera working.

### What this cannot separate

- **The images from the sentence.** The frames arm adds the photographs *and* one sentence
  announcing them. Being told there is a camera could itself raise suspicion. Two arms would
  separate these: the sentence with no images, and frames taken from a *different* run of the same
  plant. Neither is run here.
- **One view, six frames, one run per item**, as registered.
- **Distillation, not CVD**, and the blind condition is structural. The real-data registration's
  deviations 1 and 2 still apply.
